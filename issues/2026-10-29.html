Worth a look
The sun's magnetic field, mapped in 3D

Researchers pulled the first global 3D estimates of the sun's outer magnetic field out of existing Mauna Loa observatory data, by decoding waves already traveling through the corona instead of building a new instrument.

Why it's interesting: Those fields drive the solar storms that threaten power grids, so better maps mean better warnings.

Source: Phys.org

Levitating beads with sound to study static

Bristol researchers used sound waves to hold two tiny plastic balls in midair, bump them together over and over, and measure the charge after each hit. The charge built up and then leveled off, much like a capacitor charging.

Why it's interesting: Static sparks in powder handling cause explosions, and nobody fully understands how that charge builds.

Source: Phys.org

The ocean around Antarctica is getting saltier

Since sea ice crashed in 2016, a deep layer of the Southern Ocean has flipped from decades of freshening to rising salinity. That water spreads into every major ocean, carrying heat and carbon, and researchers say effects could show up within years.

Why it's interesting: It's a shift scientists usually see over millennia, happening in under a decade.

Source: Ars Technica

AI checks a 15,000-page math proof

Axiom says AI agents helped it machine-verify the Classification of Finite Simple Groups in 24 days, a result whose original proof spans about 15,000 pages. The formal version runs about 43 million lines in Lean, a proof checker.

Why it's interesting: One of math's biggest proofs, which few people could ever check by hand, can now be verified step by step by software.

Source: The Neuron
