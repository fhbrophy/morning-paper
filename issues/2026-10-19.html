Worth a look
Microplastics in water make damaging radicals on their own

A Columbia team reports in Nature that water touching common plastics (polyethylene, polypropylene, PVC, PET and others) generates hydroxyl radicals at the surface. The most water-repellent plastics were the most active, and the radicals clumped proteins and broke up mouse red blood cells in lab tests.

Why it’s interesting: It points to a possible way microplastics cause harm, and suggests plastics could be redesigned to be less reactive.

Source: Ars Technica / Nature

AI designs enzymes that cut specific proteins

David Baker’s lab used AI design tools (RFdiffusion2, ProteinMPNN) to build brand-new proteases around known catalytic cores. After tuning, the best designs sped up peptide-bond cutting by about a billion-fold while hitting only their target, including proteins linked to Alzheimer’s and dementia.

Why it’s interesting: It shows AI moving from designing protein shapes to designing working catalysts, though therapies are still far off.

Source: Ars Technica / Nature

Record-intensity attosecond X-ray pulses

European XFEL researchers focused ~300-attosecond hard X-ray flashes onto a spot 180 × 330 nanometers wide using precision mirrors, topping 1020 W/cm² — a world record for this kind of pulse, confirmed by laser-like X-ray emission from copper foil (Optica).

Why it’s interesting: Pulses this short and intense could let scientists watch, and eventually steer, electrons during chemical reactions.

Source: Phys.org / Optica
