The news
CPI lands at 6:30am AZ; gas is the wild card

September CPI comes out this morning at 8:30am ET (6:30am AZ). Economists expect headline prices up 0.6% for the month and 3.6% from a year ago (up from 3.4% in August), the biggest monthly jump since April, mostly from gasoline, which rose 7.1% in September. Core CPI is expected at +0.2% for the month and 2.5% for the year. Retail sales follow Thursday.

Why it matters: A hot print keeps pressure on the Fed and on mortgage rates; a soft core number would help.

Source: AP · Reuters via Investing.com · BLS schedule

Why it matters: Rates are still anchored above 7%, so a small dip doesn’t change the Mesa affordability picture yet.

Data centers: FERC rejects PJM’s plan; Oracle needs gas; Anthropic eyes Osaka

FERC struck down most of PJM’s proposal for managing large-load (data center) interconnections, though a separate panel approved PJM’s data center registry while refusing to let it curtail their power. Oracle asked FERC to fast-track the Green Chile gas pipeline for its Project Jupiter campus in New Mexico, now projected for Feb 2027, and is weighing trucked-in CNG in the meantime. NTT Data is reportedly close to building a ¥1 trillion (about $6.3B), up-to-500MW Osaka data center for Anthropic. Cisco and NVIDIA announced 400G-per-lane pluggable optics for AI networks.

Why it matters: Power access, not land, keeps setting the pace for new campuses, and on-site generation is becoming normal.

Source: CleanPowerDaily · Crypto Briefing · Data Centre Magazine

Phoenix leans on Menlo Digital’s Ahwatukee build

Phoenix is stationing inspectors at Menlo Digital’s million-square-foot data center site near 48th St and Thistle Landing after neighbor complaints about noise, lights, and off-hours work. Why it matters: Local pushback is growing in your own metro, but the build pipeline keeps pulling in trades and support work.

Source: Ahwatukee Foothills News · Phoenix Business Journal

Worth a look
First utility-scale enhanced geothermal plant is online

Fervo Energy’s Cape Station in Utah started selling power from its first 33 MW unit, the first next-generation geothermal plant to do it at utility scale. Fervo drills oil-and-gas style wells to create its own reservoir in hot, dry rock. Two more 33 MW blocks are due by Jan 1, 2027, and the remaining 400 MW, backed by a Google deal, by 2028. Cost is steep for now: about $7,000/kW for phase 1 versus about $2,157/kW for a gas plant.

Why it’s interesting: Firm, always-on power that can sit behind the meter is exactly what data centers are hunting for.

Source: IEEE Spectrum

A honey bee stinger is a double-piston pump

High-speed video in a new PNAS paper shows two valves inside the stinger inflate like parachutes on the downstroke and collapse on the upstroke, pumping in opposite directions for a continuous flow of venom, even after the bee flies off. A 3D-printed scale model reproduced the motion.

Why it’s interesting: A tidy bit of fluid mechanics that could inspire tiny medical and microfluidic pumps.

Source: Ars Technica

War-gaming solar geoengineering went badly

A UK-funded simulation put diplomats, scientists, military officers, and companies in a mock UN committee on dimming the sun. Suspicion between the US and China derailed every deal, a fossil-fuel-backed group quietly undermined the science adviser, and the organizers concluded that strategic competition, not climate, drove the outcome.

Why it’s interesting: A sobering look at how a powerful technology could be governed, or not.

Source: Ars Technica (from WIRED)

AI labs are buying dead startups’ data

Startup wind-down firms like SimpleClosure and Sunset now sell failed companies’ code and workspace data (Slack, Notion, email) to AI labs for training. SimpleClosure has handled 150+ deals and expects to return over $10M to founders this year; 47% of its deals now include data sales.

Why it’s interesting: A new and slightly eerie source of training data, and a privacy question for anyone who worked at a startup.

Source: MIT Technology Review
