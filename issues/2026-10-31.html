Markets: AI selloff deepens, Amazon recovers most of its drop

The Dow fell 1.1% to 51,770, the S&P 500 fell 1.8% to 7,580, and the Nasdaq dropped 2.9% to 25,437 Friday, as weak reactions to Amazon and Intel earnings fed worries about AI spending. Amazon opened near $232 after its revenue miss but closed at $240.35, down 1.4%, well better than the 6–7% premarket drop. Source: MarketWatch · Yahoo Finance · Data Center Daily

Data centers: Glendale's gas plant raises air rule questions

The Glendale data center project will be powered partly by its own 744-megawatt natural gas plant, called Project Baccara, so it doesn't have to wait on the state's crowded grid. The Arizona Republic says that raises new questions about whether regulators treat it as a data center or a power plant for air pollution rules. Elsewhere, NVIDIA is weighing a credit guarantee for Nscale's West Virginia data center, and more than a quarter of Pennsylvania's 90-plus planned projects have stalled amid local opposition.

Why it matters: Bringing your own power is becoming the Valley model, which means more onsite generation and the engineers to run it.

Source: Arizona Republic · TechCrunch · Data Center Record

A supersonic jet maker pivots to data center turbines

Boom Supersonic is putting more weight on Superpower, a 42-megawatt natural gas turbine for AI data centers, with first deliveries targeted for mid-2027 from a Colorado factory still under construction. United's CEO now doubts Boom's airliner will be ready by 2029, and its first turbine customer, Crusoe, dropped a $1.25 billion order.

Why it's interesting: Data center power demand is pulling in companies from far outside the energy business.

Source: Ars Technica

Data centers built on an assembly line

Startup Runware wants to factory-build 1- and 2-megawatt containerized data center pods with their own cooling, power and networking, then ship them ready to plug in. It's aiming for up to 30 megawatts a day once production starts in early 2027, which is still unproven.

Why it's interesting: If it works, more of the electrical and cooling work moves into a factory and less happens on site.

Source: TechCrunch

A 4.1-billion-year-old piece of Mars

A meteorite called Teghaza 001, found in the Sahara in Mali in 2022, turns out to be a slowly cooled chunk of Mars's ancient crust at least 4.1 billion years old, one of the oldest Martian rocks known. Its chemistry suggests early Mars had water actively circulating.

Why it's interesting: Very few rocks survive from that early, and this one came to us for free.

Source: Ars Technica
