Worth a look
One antimatter particle, held still for months

Northwestern physicists caught a single positron from radioactive sodium-22, cooled it to about 0.3 degrees above absolute zero, and floated it in a wall-free electric and magnetic trap for months. They can now nudge it between quantum energy levels with microwaves.

Why it's interesting: Next they'll measure its magnetism and compare it with the electron. Any mismatch would be a crack in the Standard Model and a clue to why the universe is made of matter.

Source: Phys.org

The shortest hard X-ray pulses ever measured

SLAC's LCLS laser proved it is making 9 keV X-ray pulses just 100 to 400 attoseconds long, using fluorescence from a copper foil to time them. Researchers have already used them to freeze dense plasma states before they change.

Why it's interesting: They're fast enough to capture electrons moving and to outrun the damage the X-rays themselves cause.

Source: Phys.org

SpaceX will try to catch both Starship stages next flight

On its earnings call, SpaceX said Flight 15, targeted for November, will try tower catches of both the Super Heavy booster and the Starship upper stage. Musk is aiming for weekly launches in early 2027, alongside up to 10GW of ground-based AI compute.

Why it's interesting: Catching the upper stage over land is a first, and it's the key to the airplane-style reuse SpaceX is betting on.

Source: Ars Technica
