Worth a look
Seabirds get jet lag, and it bends their compass

Twenty years of GPS tracks show Manx shearwaters flying home across the Atlantic drift off course by up to about 15 degrees, because their internal clock lags behind local time and they navigate by the sun. They correct along the way, so it adds only a few kilometers.

Why it's interesting: It's a real-world look at how a clock error turns into a navigation error, something any mariner would recognize.

Source: Ars Technica

NASA asks again for ideas to save Hubble

Without a boost, Hubble will likely fall out of orbit in 2033 or 2034. NASA is asking companies how they'd capture it and raise it to at least 600 km, service it, or bring it down safely, with responses due Nov. 30.

Why it's interesting: A commercial rescue of a 36-year-old telescope would be a big test of private space servicing.

Source: Ars Technica

Google's tiny data center in orbit is running

Google's Suncatcher prototype satellite carries four TPU chips on about 1 kilowatt of power and has started running AI in orbit. Next it tests how the chips hold up to heat and radiation.

Why it's interesting: Space is about the only place with no permitting or grid wait, though it's very early days.

Source: The Morning Build
