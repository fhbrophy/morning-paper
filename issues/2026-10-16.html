NERC advances reliability rules for large AI data centers

On Oct 15, NERC stakeholders passed a final ballot on reliability standards for computational loads (AI data centers, crypto, etc.) of ≥50 MW at ≥100 kV. Next: NERC board, then FERC. Rules cover interconnection studies/modeling, operational data and communications, and disturbance monitoring — after a May Level 3 alert on abrupt load drops. NERC has flagged ~224 GW of summer peak growth over 10 years, with data centers a major driver.

Worth a look
Microplastics generate damaging radicals in water

Columbia chemists showed that common plastics in water spontaneously produce hydroxyl radicals at the plastic–water interface. All six plastics tested did it; the most water-repellent (PE, PP) were most active. Radicals were tied to protein aggregation and damage to mouse red blood cells near the particle surface. Human disease link is still open.

Why it’s interesting: Overturns the “plastics are chemically inert” assumption and points to a surface-chemistry lever.

Source: Ars Technica

Prima implant restores reading vision in advanced AMD

The Prima system (Science Corporation) is a 378-pixel photovoltaic retinal implant plus camera glasses and a pocket processor. It replaces dead photoreceptors and feeds healthy bipolar/ganglion cells via near-IR pulses. Approved in Europe; awaiting FDA. After training, more than 80% of recipients could read, do crosswords, and use phones.

Why it’s interesting: Clean engineering — camera → IR projector → solar-powered implant — restoring a core daily ability.

Source: MIT Technology Review

AI designs “dirty alloys” from scrap impurities

Max Planck researchers used ML to treat scrap impurities as useful alloying elements instead of diluting them with virgin metal. On ductile iron, 14 of 17 AI-suggested designs beat the recycled-iron strength–ductility frontier (up to +15% tensile strength or +73% elongation), with manufacturing costs ~8% lower than the conventional scrap+virgin blend.

Why it’s interesting: Practical materials AI for circular industry and lower-carbon metal supply.

Source: Phys.org

Superfluid helium sensor for Earth’s frame-dragging

UC Berkeley / Caltech theorists propose a superfluid helium-4 gyrometer (doughnut loop + nanopore Josephson junction) cooled to ~10 mK to detect Einstein’s frame-dragging — Earth dragging spacetime as it rotates — with sensitivity that could match or beat prior space-based measurements, but on a lab bench.

Why it’s interesting: Tabletop general relativity with a macroscopic quantum fluid.

Source: Phys.org
