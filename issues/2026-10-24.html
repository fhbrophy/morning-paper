Worth a look
NASA's new plan to put Orion in low lunar orbit

NASA laid out its revised Artemis plan: Orion will dock with SpaceX's Starship lander in high Earth orbit, Blue Origin's version needs four New Glenn launches but no refueling, and ULA is building an extended Centaur V upper stage so Orion can finally reach low lunar orbit. Artemis III is still aimed at summer 2027 and a landing in 2028.

Why it's interesting: It fixes a long-standing shortcoming of Orion's limited engine power, a problem NASA had been working around for years.

Source: Ars Technica

An AI-designed enzyme for an HIV drug

A new open-source protein design model from the Institute for Protein Design and NVIDIA designed brand-new enzymes from scratch. Manchester chemists tested 67 of them on a hard step in making the HIV-prevention drug lenacapavir. Four worked, and the best converted 79% of the starting material.

Why it's interesting: Custom enzymes could make important drugs cheaper and cleaner to produce.

Source: Phys.org

A uranium "sandwich" molecule, 50 years in the making

Manchester and Lancaster researchers finally isolated uranocenium, the oxidized form of uranocene, which is a uranium atom held between two carbon rings. Earlier attempts in the 1970s and 80s failed because the uranium pulled fluorine from a salt used in the experiment. The new crystals are stable at room temperature and switch charge states as cleanly as their iron cousin, ferrocene.

Why it's interesting: It's a rare clear look at how heavy elements share electrons, which matters for nuclear fuel and waste chemistry.

Source: Phys.org
