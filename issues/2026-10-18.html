AI: FTC probes OpenAI and Anthropic on safety incidents

The FTC sent civil investigative demands to OpenAI and Anthropic seeking extensive records on AI safety incidents, safeguards, privacy, and marketing, with possible third-party subpoenas and executive testimony. The probe follows reports of AI agents improperly accessing systems.

Why it matters: Regulatory heat is rising even as compute spend continues — another reason markets are debating AI infrastructure valuations.

Source: Axios · CBS News

Data centers: Oregon pause deepens; Ukraine hits Yandex AI sites

Oregon Gov. Tina Kotek’s halt on new or expanded data-center air and water permits runs through July 1, 2027, and Hillsboro is moving toward a permanent acreage ceiling. Separately, Ukrainian drones damaged Yandex data centers in Sasovo and Kaluga, including a site used to train YandexGPT — another sign that AI facilities are wartime targets.

Why it matters: Permitting and geopolitics both shape where (and how safely) hyperscale campuses get built and staffed.

Source: The Oregonian · Ars Technica

Worth a look
Google cuts quantum “magic state” errors 40× on real hardware

Google Quantum AI demonstrated magic state cultivation on its Willow superconducting processor, cutting errors by a factor of about 40 and reaching roughly 99.99% fidelity. Only about 8% of attempts passed every check. Published in Science.

Why it’s interesting: A practical step toward fewer spare qubits for the special states universal quantum computers need.

Source: Phys.org / Science

Chip-scale optical gyroscope senses 37°/hour

UC Santa Barbara and Caltech built a CMOS-compatible Brillouin ring-laser gyroscope on silicon nitride that measures rotation as slow as 37 degrees per hour — nearly 2,000× better than their earlier chip design, and approaching Earth’s 15°/hour rate.

Why it’s interesting: Precision navigation without GPS, in a rugged chip with no moving parts.

Source: Phys.org / Optics Letters

“Carpet” growth unlocks a true 2D topological insulator

UChicago engineers grew millimeter-scale, uniform two-layer bismuth telluride films that spectroscopically confirm a long-sought 2D topological insulator — edge channels that should conduct with little scattering. ACS Nano.

Why it’s interesting: A materials breakthrough that could eventually mean nearly lossless edge wiring for electronics.

Source: Phys.org / ACS Nano

When AI data centers become wartime targets

Ars Technica details how Ukrainian drones knocked out Yandex AI data centers used for model training, part of a broader 2026 pattern of strikes on compute facilities. Worth reading for the ops and resilience angle, not just the geopolitics.

Why it’s interesting: Critical-facilities work increasingly includes physical and power resilience assumptions that didn’t matter five years ago.
