The news
Democrats take Congress, and markets bounce back

Democrats won majorities in both the House and Senate. Nasdaq-100 futures fell as much as 2.4% overnight, then reversed after Trump said he would accept the results and work with Democrats. By early morning Dow futures were up about 1.4%, with S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 futures up about 1%. Amazon was up about 1.1% premarket, while Tesla, SpaceX, and CoreWeave fell. The 10-year Treasury yield dipped to about 4.91%, the dollar hit a one-month low, and gold rose above $4,000.

Why it matters: Investors are now watching for tougher AI and data center rules under a Democratic Congress, which matters for Amazon stock and your RSUs.

Source: CNBC · Reuters via USA Today · AP

Job market: openings jumped in September

Job openings rose by 333,000 to 7.52 million, well above the roughly 7.1 million economists expected. Layoffs fell to their lowest in a year and a half, and quits rose to the highest level since early 2025, which means more workers feel confident enough to switch jobs. Construction openings rose 36,000, which Reuters tied partly to the AI buildout. Friday's October jobs report is next.

Why it matters: More openings and more people quitting usually means more movement in the job market, which helps someone looking to switch.

Source: BLS · CNBC · Reuters via Investing.com

Data centers: the backlash showed up at the polls

Axios reports voters pushed back on the AI buildout in several races. Democrats won Ohio, Georgia, and Arizona, three of the four biggest states for new data center capacity after Texas, and the winners in Ohio and Georgia favor tighter rules. In Arizona, Gov. Katie Hobbs won re-election about 56% to 42%, and she has said a statewide data center moratorium isn't the right approach. Locally, Oro Valley has banned stand-alone data centers, and Pima County is in a 120-day pause on new applications.

Why it matters: Phoenix-area projects look safer from a statewide pause than other markets do, but local rules keep getting tighter.

Source: Axios · Bisnow · Tucson Local Media

AI: OpenAI shows engineering work for physical products

OpenAI says its models now help design, simulate, and build hardware. Why it matters: AI tools are starting to do real electrical and mechanical design work, not just writing code.

Worth a look
Storing summer sun as iron powder for winter

German researchers modeled using green hydrogen to turn rust back into iron powder, storing it for months, then burning it in retrofitted coal plants for winter power and heat. Burning iron releases no CO2, and the rust it leaves can be recycled again. They estimate about 83 GW of Europe's old coal capacity could be reused this way by 2045.

Why it's interesting: It's a seasonal storage idea that reuses existing turbines and grid connections.

Source: Tech Xplore · TU Darmstadt

A 42-year particle hunt gets close at CERN

CERN's LHCb experiment found the first single-experiment evidence of a decay that happens about once in every 10 billion B mesons, a search that began in 1984. The result is strong evidence but not yet a confirmed discovery, and so far it matches the Standard Model.

Why it's interesting: Decays this rare are one of the best places to spot physics nobody has found yet.

Source: University of Cambridge · University of Manchester

AI that catches errors between drawings and calcs

A Seoul team built a pipeline that pulls data from structural calculation documents and drawings and cross-checks them for mismatched dimensions, missing entries, and duplicates. On one residential project it flagged 253 discrepancies, 29 of them critical, without needing a BIM model.

Why it's interesting: The same idea could catch mismatches between electrical one-lines, schedules, and as-builts.

Source: Tech Xplore
