Worth a look
An atomic clock that survives a ship at sea

Infleqtion ran its Tiqker optical atomic clock aboard the Navy support vessel RV Merlin for more than two weeks during a Pacific exercise off Southern California, testing timing that doesn't depend on GPS through temperature swings and constant vibration. No accuracy figures were released.

Why it's interesting: Shipboard timing without GPS is exactly the kind of gear you used to keep running.

Source: Infleqtion

Cold fusion isn't all fraud

Quanta looks at muon-catalyzed fusion, which really does fuse hydrogen at room temperature in the lab. It can't make power because each muon decays in about 2.2 microseconds and only manages 100 to 150 reactions, but Japanese researchers recently confirmed a key step of the theory.

Why it's interesting: It's a clear explanation of why something can be physically real and still useless as an energy source.

Source: Quanta Magazine

Can peer review keep up with AI-written papers?

A writer tried to sneak AI-generated papers past IEEE. Human reviewers plus integrity-checking software caught the sloppy ones from free tools, but stronger models no longer make the obvious mistakes.

Why it's interesting: It's an honest look at an arms race that affects every engineering journal.

Source: IEEE Spectrum

Quantum error correction finally scales

Quantinuum showed that each step up in logical qubit size cut errors by roughly a factor of 12, the best scaling reported so far.

Why it's interesting: It's the first sign that error correction can get quantum computers to useful reliability without absurd hardware counts.

Source: Ars Technica
