Worth a look
AI's quasi-Riemann proof holds up with the experts

Quanta reports that number theorists studying OpenAI's proof of the "quasi-Riemann hypothesis" (that the zeta function's zeros sit in a narrower strip than previously proven) largely agree it's correct. It works by combining old ideas in a way nobody expected, and one mathematician called the implications "staggering," since most consequences of the full Riemann hypothesis follow from this weaker version.

Why it's interesting: It's the clearest case yet of AI cracking a major open math problem, though the full hypothesis still looks out of reach.

Source: Quanta Magazine

First power from an undersea volcano

Seattle startup Endurance Energy ran a 100kW prototype geothermal plant about 5,000 feet down at the Axial Seamount off the Pacific Northwest. It made more power than it used for about 8.7 seconds (peak 16.5kW net) before a battery-sensor fault and a seawater-shorted cable ended the run. A 2MW, container-sized unit is planned for Tonga in 2027.

Why it's interesting: It's a real-world look at the marine engineering involved: seafloor wells, turbines and subsea cabling, and how one bad cable jacket ends a test.

Source: Ars Technica

An 11-foot Chinese rocket stage landed in a Minnesota cornfield

A farmer harvesting in Stevens County, Minnesota found an 11-foot metal ring that matches the upper stage of a Chinese Zhuque-2E rocket. It launched Sept 15, dropped 10 satellites, then came down over the Midwest the same day, likely after a botched deorbit burn that should have steered it to the ocean.

Why it's interesting: Debris this big rarely survives reentry, and under international law the launching country is liable for any damage.

Source: Ars Technica
