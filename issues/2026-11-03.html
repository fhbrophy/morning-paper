Data centers: SRP weighs a pause

SRP board members asked staff Monday about options for curbing new data center connections. One director said about 3,000 of the 4,700 megawatts SRP plans to add by 2035 are for data centers. SRP's lawyer said a moratorium is unlikely because state law requires SRP to serve eligible customers, and staff pointed to tariffs that make data centers pay up front for 80% of their peak demand. In Glendale, the $36 billion Project Eagle campus near Luke Air Force Base is cleared to start construction this month.

Why it matters: SRP is your utility, and this debate decides both your power bill and how fast Valley data centers can grow.

Source: KJZZ · AZFamily

Worth a look
Why big dogs age faster, from ASU

Arizona State researchers studied DNA from 864 dogs and found giant breeds lose about 35% more of the chemical control over "jumping genes" each year than small breeds. Those genes can damage DNA as they move, which lines up with big dogs' shorter lives.

Why it's interesting: It's a local lab with one of the clearest explanations yet for the size and lifespan puzzle.

Source: Ars Technica

One antimatter particle, trapped for months

Northwestern physicists held a single positron in a vacuum trap chilled to 0.3 degrees above absolute zero for months, and could read its quantum state with tuned microwaves. Next they want to measure its magnetism precisely and compare it to the electron's.

Why it's interesting: Any difference between the two would be, in the lead researcher's words, the biggest physics discovery in a century.

Source: Ars Technica

A single molecule as a quantum network node

A German startup built a qubit from a single custom molecule held in a crystal at 4.5 kelvin. It kept its quantum state for about 2.2 milliseconds, and its light output stayed stable for over an hour, which is the hard part for linking qubits with light.

Why it's interesting: Chemists can design these from scratch, unlike flaws in diamond.

Source: Phys.org

PHP2Shell and the cost of old code

IEEE Spectrum looks at PHP2Shell, a widespread web vulnerability found with AI help, and argues AI is forcing the industry to pay off 40 years of technical debt as it finds old bugs faster than people can fix them.

Why it's interesting: It's a sign of how AI is changing security work for the systems data centers run.

Source: IEEE Spectrum
