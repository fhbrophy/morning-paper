September CPI rose 0.6% for the month and 3.7% year over year (vs. 3.4% in August), hotter than the 3.6% annual expectation. Core CPI was +0.3% / 2.6% (vs. 0.2% / 2.5% expected). Worth a look
Pig kidneys frozen for a year work again

University of Minnesota researchers vitrified pig kidneys to about −150°C (one stored over a year), then rewarmed them with iron-oxide nanoparticles and an electromagnetic field. Rewarmed kidneys pinked up, made urine on perfusion machines and in short pig transplants — not life-sustaining yet, but a milestone toward organ banking.

Why it’s interesting: A path toward organs waiting for patients instead of the other way around.

Source: MIT Technology Review

AI designs proteases that cut chosen proteins

David Baker’s lab used RFdiffusion2 and ProteinMPNN to redesign zinc and cysteine proteases around a fixed catalytic core, then tuned them to cut specific sequences — including proteins tied to neurological disease. Activity started low but jumped orders of magnitude after mutation screening near the active site.

Why it’s interesting: Structure design is racing ahead of catalytic finesse — and therapies would still need delivery.

Source: Ars Technica

Perseverance breaks Opportunity’s Mars distance record

On Oct 11, Perseverance’s odometer hit 45.22 km (28.10 miles), passing Opportunity’s 45.16 km record. AutoNav now handles about 90% of driving (vs. ~5% for Opportunity/Curiosity), with speeds up to ~120 m/hour — still with humans setting keep-out zones from orbit.

Why it’s interesting: A clean demonstration of how much autonomy changes what a robot can cover.

Source: IEEE Spectrum

How much more can existing nukes give?

Constellation plans $4.3B+ in uprates across 11 reactors (~enough for 700,000 homes) with Google as a buyer; DOE is backing Vistra upgrades Meta will offtake. U.S. nuclear capacity is ~98 GW; realistic uprate headroom is only a few GW — a fraction of PJM’s expected ~30 GW of AI-driven peak demand by 2030.

Why it’s interesting: Firm clean power for data centers, but uprates alone won’t close the gap.

Source: MIT Technology Review
