AI: Anthropic files to go public

Anthropic made its IPO filing public Monday. Worth a look
A 1,000-mile cloud on Mars runs on "textbook-only" physics

Every Martian spring morning a thin cloud forms by the Arsia Mons volcano and stretches over 1,000 miles. A new Nature Geoscience model explains it with homogeneous nucleation, where vapor freezes straight into ice without dust to form on. It has never been seen in nature, and it needs a 30°C drop in 10 minutes.

Why it's interesting: It's like condensation forming in the middle of a room instead of on the window.

Source: Ars Technica

The next ice age started 2,000 years ago, until we canceled it

Moss frozen under Baffin Island's Barnes Ice Cap, the last piece of the ice sheet that once covered Canada, shows the ice began growing again about 2,000 years ago. The study in Science finds it kept advancing until about 1960, when warming reversed it. Barnes is now melting everywhere and should be gone within a few centuries.

Why it's interesting: Dead moss turns out to be a precise clock for when the ice arrived.

Source: Ars Technica

Fiber optics made in orbit come out clearer

ZBLAN fiber drawn on the ISS lost far less light than ground-made fiber (0.3–1.2 dB per meter versus 2–7 at 1.55 µm), because microgravity keeps crystals from forming. Impurities are still the main obstacle to making it worth the trip.

Why it's interesting: It's one of the few products where manufacturing in space might actually pay off.

Source: IEEE Spectrum · EurekAlert

Laser-blasted plasma hints at why the sun's corona is so hot

Lab experiments that blast plasma with lasers show how instabilities could heat the sun's outer atmosphere, a long-running puzzle because the corona is far hotter than the surface below it.

Why it's interesting: Shows how lab experiments can stand in for the sun.

Source: Phys.org
