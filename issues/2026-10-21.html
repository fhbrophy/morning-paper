Worth a look
Quantum error correction finally scales the way it needs to

Quantinuum's Helios trapped-ion machine built error-corrected "logical" qubits from 13, 37 and 73 physical qubits. Each step up cut the memory error rate by about 12 times, from about 5 in 10,000 down to 5 in a million, which Ars calls the best scaling seen yet. A laser trick that pulls stray ions back into usable states cut one major error source by 6.5 times.

Why it's interesting: Earlier demos only roughly halved errors per step, which implied impractically huge machines. Tenfold-plus gains mean useful quantum computers may need far fewer qubits.

Source: Ars Technica

An under-skin implant that runs a prosthetic arm

Austin startup Phantom Neuro implanted a 2-by-10-centimeter electrode strip under the forearm skin of two patients in Melbourne. It reads muscle signals and wirelessly drives off-the-shelf prosthetic hands. The surgery took under an hour as an outpatient procedure, and calibration lasts months instead of the daily recalibration skin-surface sensors need.

Why it's interesting: It's a neat bit of practical engineering: inductive power, sealed electronics and a wireless link, without brain surgery. The company hopes for FDA approval in two to three years.

Source: IEEE Spectrum

CERN and Mistral plan a "Large Physics Model"

CERN and French AI lab Mistral signed a statement of intent to build the first foundation model for collider physics, trained on billions of simulated proton collisions from the Large Hadron Collider. One model would handle particle reconstruction and identification, flag anomalies and help evaluate detector designs.

Why it's interesting: It's the same "one big pretrained model" idea behind chatbots, aimed at finding new physics in the world's largest machine.

Source: AIBriefs
