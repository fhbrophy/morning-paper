Worth a look
A semiconductor with a record 7-volt bandgap

Cornell researchers grew silicon-doped aluminum gallium oxide on cheap sapphire and got it to conduct at bandgaps above 7 eV, beating the old record. Above 6 eV it conducted over 100 million times better than earlier attempts, and working diodes and transistors were built from it.

Why it's interesting: Wider bandgaps handle higher voltages with less heat, so this could lead to more efficient power electronics, the kind that fill data center electrical rooms.

Source: Phys.org

A personalized mRNA cancer vaccine works in a big trial

Moderna and Merck's custom mRNA vaccine, added to Keytruda, cut the risk of melanoma coming back or death by 42% in a phase 3 trial of 1,137 patients. About 82% stayed recurrence-free at two years, compared with 70% on Keytruda alone.

Why it's interesting: It's the first late-stage win for a vaccine built from each patient's own tumor.

Source: Ars Technica

Black holes kicked out of their galaxies' centers

Across about 100,000 quasars, astronomers found that faster-moving central black holes are more often hidden by dust, a pattern predicted a decade ago for black holes knocked loose by the recoil of a merger and swinging back and forth through the core like a pendulum.

Why it's interesting: If it holds, black hole mergers are common, which means plenty for the LISA gravitational-wave observatory to catch after 2035.

Source: Phys.org
