Data centers: Oregon pauses permits; Microsoft locks 1.1 GW in Indiana

Oregon Gov. Tina Kotek ordered a time-limited pause on air and water pollution permits for new or expanding data centers, effectively halting new development there. NiSource signed a 20-year deal for about 1,100 MW of new generation for Microsoft’s northwest Indiana campus from 2028, with Microsoft funding generation and transmission and 600 MW of battery storage added. Anthropic signed its first Canadian data center lease with EverWind in Nova Scotia.

Why it matters: Customer-funded power and state-level pushback are now shaping where hyperscale campuses (and critical-facilities jobs) land.

Worth a look
AI designs working protein “scissors” from scratch

David Baker’s lab at the University of Washington used RFdiffusion2, ProteinMPNN and AlphaFold 3 to design new proteases that recognize and cut chosen protein sequences. 13 of 69 cysteine designs and 36% of 135 zinc designs worked; refinement made the best one 30 times faster. Some cut targets linked to ALS, Alzheimer’s and amyloidosis. Two Nature papers.

Why it’s interesting: A jump from AI-designed shapes to AI-designed enzymes that actually do a precise job.

Source: Phys.org

The gallium neutrino anomaly may be solved without new particles

For years gallium experiments caught about 20% fewer electron neutrinos than predicted, fueling theories about “sterile” neutrinos. Italian physicists redid the calculation, treating the electron and nuclear transition together instead of separately, and the prediction now matches what experiments see. Published in Physical Review Letters.

Why it’s interesting: A classic case of a better calculation dissolving a supposed sign of new physics.

Source: Phys.org

Record-intensity attosecond X-ray pulses

At the European XFEL, researchers focused roughly 300-attosecond hard X-ray pulses to a 180 × 330 nanometer spot using precision mirrors, topping 1020 W/cm². They confirmed it by making a copper foil emit amplified forward X-ray light.

Why it’s interesting: Pulses this short and intense could let scientists watch, and eventually steer, electrons during chemical reactions.

Source: Phys.org
