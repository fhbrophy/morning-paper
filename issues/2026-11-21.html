The news
AI and economy: a brutal week for AI stocks

The Nasdaq fell 10% this week, its worst since March 2020, and the S&P 500 and Dow both closed Friday in correction (down 10% from highs). Anthropic, which gained 17% on its debut, fell more than 8% Friday to close near its IPO price. Nvidia reported Q3 revenue of $115.8B (up 103%) with data center revenue of $107.6B and guided Q4 to $130B, yet shares kept sliding on a six-day losing streak. NPR points to the September Fed hike, new Democratic majorities pushing tougher AI and data-center policy, and the Nvidia CEO turmoil.

Why it matters: Your RSU value and the AI hiring picture both ride on this trade.

Source: NPR · Nvidia

Mortgage rates: Freddie Mac 30-year at 7.28%

The 30-year fixed averaged 7.28% as of Nov 19 (15-year 6.66%), up from 7.11% last week and 6.26% a year ago. Realtor.com ties the jump to hot October CPI, the 10-year near 5%, and expectations of a December hike.

Why it matters: Rates are moving the wrong way for buyers.

Source: Freddie Mac · Realtor.com

Job market: layoffs low, hiring modest

Initial jobless claims were 203,000 for the week ended Nov 14, with the 4-week average at 197,750. Continuing claims rose 70,000 to 1.763M, mostly from a Michigan system change. Reuters calls it a “medium-hire, low-fire” market, and median unemployment duration hit 11.9 weeks in October. Indeed postings are at their highest since April 2025.

Source: Dept. of Labor · Reuters · Macro Mostly

Data centers: Ahwatukee claim and Arizona politics

Ahwatukee residents filed an $11M notice of claim against Phoenix over Menlo Digital’s 257-MW, five-building campus, a step before a possible lawsuit within 180 days. Axios says new Democratic legislative majorities are likely to take up data-center restrictions in January, with the AG pushing a moratorium and Gov. Hobbs opposed. An NPR piece notes the bigger water issue is power generation, not cooling.

Why it matters: Local backlash is the main risk to Phoenix-area data-center hiring.

Source: KTAR · Axios Phoenix · NPR

Mesa housing: buyer’s market

Mesa’s Cromford index crossed into buyer’s territory in late October (86.6, below 90). The Valley-wide index fell from 77.6 to 68.2 over 30 days, with active listings up about 8% year over year and contracts down about 18%. About 22% of contracts fell through.

Source: Inside the Valley · Mesa Tribune
